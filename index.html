Packing Context Sheet

The practical "why" behind what to bring for an expedition cruise in the tropics in late February

80°F+Daytime temps
HighHumidity
DrySeason (Pacific side)
~9°NNear the equator
What the tour expects: Smithsonian says to expect temperatures over 80°F, high humidity and strong sun, with time on small open boats that give no shade. Landings are "wet" — you step from the Zodiac into ankle-deep water — and rainforest trails can be uneven and slippery, with walks of two to three miles.

Zodiacs & wet landings

👡
Water shoes or strapped sport sandalsYou'll step into the surf at Playa Muerto and San Blas. Flip-flops slip off; closed-toe water shoes protect against coral and rocks.
🎒
Small dry bagKeeps your phone, camera and passport copy dry on Zodiac rides and spray.
🩳
Quick-dry shorts or roll-up pantsHems get wet on landings. Synthetics dry in an hour; cotton stays damp all day.
🥾
Trail shoes with gripFor the Manuel Antonio walk, the Darién waterfall hike and Pipeline Road in Gamboa.

Sun, heat & bugs

👒
Wide-brim hat with a chin strapOpen boats have no shade, and the strap keeps it on at Zodiac speed.
🧴
Reef-safe sunscreenFor snorkeling at Cayos Holandeses on the San Blas reef — mineral (zinc) formulas are kinder to coral.
🦟
Insect repellentMangrove estuaries, the Darién forest and Gamboa are prime mosquito territory, especially at dusk.
👕
Long-sleeve sun shirtCovers you from sun and bugs at once, and is cooler than it sounds.
🧥
Light rain shellRainforests earn their name, even in the dry season. Also handy against strong ship air conditioning.

Wildlife watching

🔭
BinocularsSloths, toucans and monkeys sit high in the canopy. This is the single most useful item for Manuel Antonio, Gatun Lake and Pipeline Road.
📷
Camera with zoom, or phone lens clipThe Canal transit is a full day of photo moments; wildlife needs reach.
🤿
Your own snorkel mask (optional)The ship's marina has complimentary snorkel gear, but a mask that fits you is more comfortable.

Money & practical

💵
Small US billsPanama uses the US dollar. Emberá and Guna artisans sell baskets, carvings and molas for cash, and change is scarce in villages.
🔌
No adapter neededBoth countries use the same plug shape as the US.
🛂
Passport valid 6+ monthsPONANT recommends six months beyond the end of the cruise, with blank pages for stamps.
💉
Ask your doctor about yellow feverPONANT notes the vaccine is recommended for Panama east of the Canal Zone — which includes Darién. Check CDC guidance well ahead.

Village visits

📸
Ask before photographing people"¿Puedo tomar una foto?" Some Guna communities ask for a small fee per photo.
🧺
Room in your bag for craftsEmberá woven baskets and Guna molas are flat or light, but bring a spare tote.
👗
Modest, easy layersShorts are fine; a light cover-up is respectful when you're a guest in someone's village.

Leave at home

❌
Heavy jeans and cottonToo hot and won't dry in the humidity.
❌
Wheeled bag for village daysBring a soft daypack; you'll be climbing in and out of Zodiacs.
❌
Lots of formal wearExpedition cruises are relaxed; a smart-casual outfit or two covers dinners.

What You'll See at Each Stop

A quick preview of every destination, so nothing comes as a surprise

1
San José → Puerto Caldera
Feb 18–19 · Arrival & embarkation
  • Night at the DoubleTree Cariari, near SJO airport
  • Hospitality suite and group coach to the port
  • Board Le Lapérouse at the mouth of the Gulf of Nicoya
The drive crosses Costa Rica's Central Valley down to the Pacific. The ship has an underwater lounge, the Blue Eye, with windows below the waterline.
2
Quepos & Manuel Antonio
Feb 20 · Costa Rica
  • Naturalist walk in Manuel Antonio National Park
  • Or a riverboat through the Damas Island mangroves
  • Or rafting on the Savegre River
One of Costa Rica's smallest national parks, where rainforest runs straight down to white-sand beaches. Watch for sloths, monkeys, toucans and caimans.
3
Playa Muerto · Darién
Feb 22 · After a day at sea
  • Zodiac landing at an Emberá village
  • Welcome by the chief, traditional dances
  • Optional short hike to a waterfall
Darién is Panama's most remote province and a UNESCO World Heritage site since the 1980s. About 30,000 Indigenous people live here, mostly Emberá.
4
Panama City
Feb 23 · From Fuerte Amador
  • Biomuseo, designed by Frank Gehry
  • STRI's Punta Culebra Nature Center with Smithsonian staff
  • Choice: Casco Antiguo walk and lunch, or Panamá Viejo ruins
Founded in 1519, burned in 1671, rebuilt on a new site. Both old cities are a joint UNESCO World Heritage site.
5
Panama Canal transit
Feb 24 · Full daylight crossing
  • Three levels of locks, Pacific to Caribbean
  • Gatun Lake, once a jungle valley
  • Expert lectures on the canal's history
The French began in 1881 and failed; the United States finished it between 1904 and 1914. Panama has run it since 1999.
6
San Blas Islands
Feb 25 · Cayos Holandeses
  • Swimming and snorkeling behind a coral reef
  • Meeting the Guna people
  • Molas — layered, hand-sewn textile panels
More than 300 islands governed by the Guna as an autonomous territory, Guna Yala.
E
Colón · Agua Clara · San Lorenzo
Feb 26 · Extension day 1
  • Agua Clara Observation Center over the new expanded locks
  • Ruins of Fort San Lorenzo above the Chagres River
  • Transfer to Gamboa Rainforest Reserve
San Lorenzo guarded Spain's treasure route across the isthmus; it's part of a UNESCO site with Portobelo.
E
Gamboa
Feb 27 · Extension day 2
  • Boat cruise on Gatun Lake among the islands
  • Sloth sanctuary, butterfly garden, orchids, frog pond
  • Choice: Pipeline Road birding or the aerial tram
Gamboa sits where the Chagres River meets the canal, inside Soberanía National Park. Barro Colorado Island, STRI's famous research station, is in the same lake.
